MANUFACTURING / CHANGE THE PART, NOT JUST THE SUPPLIER

The replacement did not have to be more of the same.

A recurring brittle-part problem became a Fusion model, a TPU print, and an installed CNC tool-rack component. My September 2023 post describes the geometric difficulty and what I observed after a 90-day trial.

This case has its own drawing, saved file variants, comparison photographs, printing footage, and machine footage. The story is the connection between them.

01 / MATERIAL & TIMING

The pattern came after the pour.

In a June 2020 update, I described returning about 12 hours after a pour, once the epoxy had gelled. The higher viscosity let me draw through the river with a tongue depressor and leave the swirl pattern visible in the photograph.

That timing belongs to this recorded pour, not a universal resin recipe. The useful detail is that the finished appearance involved another deliberate operation after pouring.

02 / FIT & REQUIREMENTS

A small object can carry a specific brief.

The RV board had exact dimensions and threaded hardware for reinforcement and adjustment. The hanging shelves offered two standard sizes and custom variations. The plant enclosure began with the surroundings it had to work in.

These are not extra disciplines to count. They are the actual decisions that make each object worth a closer look.

03 / REPAIR & ADAPTATION

Replace the part, not the whole thing.

The wardrobe repair starts with a cracked fitting and ends with a printed replacement installed on the existing rails. The jar lid is another small interface problem, and the restored chair frames approach reuse through refurbishment instead of replacement.

These pages belong together as an editorial route through repairs and adaptations, not as a claim that they were a single project.

05 / COMMISSION & COMPOSITION

The brief can leave room for interpretation.

The double-sided board began with a budget and an image rather than a full fabrication plan. Its caption describes intended uses for the two faces, contrasting woods, a cherry frame, and homemade walnut plugs.

That is a richer account than a single label such as cutting board: it preserves what was requested and the choices that turned the request into a particular object.
