A plant and a workshop wanted different conditions

The original brief came from several competing needs: plant-eating cats, a deliberately dry office used for printing, and difficulty maintaining a stable indoor climate through Michigan seasons. I wanted a way to keep a plant in that setting without treating the room and the plant as though they needed identical conditions.

Make the enclosure, not another room

Printed PETG parts and glass covers created a localized enclosure around the plant. It was my first complete Fusion-model-to-print project, and the original post describes multiple iterations before I was satisfied.

The useful design move was to put the boundary around the specific object that needed it. This links the plant problem to the practical work of modeling interfaces and making parts that fit existing covers.

What I would keep improving

The enclosure worked well enough to photograph and use, but I could already see places I wanted to improve. I kept iterating because this was my first complete Fusion-to-print project.

Working from the room conditions

The plants were in a dry workshop and office, and the cats were another part of the problem. I modeled and printed PETG bases that worked with glass covers to create a more contained space around each plant. This was my first full Fusion-to-print project, so I went through several versions and could already see improvements I wanted to make. The finished photos show the version I reached, not a claim that I had solved every growing condition.
