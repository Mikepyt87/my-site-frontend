Multicam tool fork: redesigning a part that kept breaking

The original part kept breaking. I rebuilt its geometry in Fusion, changed the material to TPU, and put the printed replacement to work in the machine it was made for.

September 2023Reverse engineering / field trial

The problem

Brittle original tool forks, recurring breakage, and a reported replacement price of $45–$65 each.

My response

Reverse-engineer the curved geometry, make targeted adjustments, and print a TPU replacement on my Voron V0.2.

The reported result

Firm retention after a 90-day trial, survival of the impacts described in my report, and a stated $35 replacement cost.

Professional notes +

From a maintenance problem to an implemented component

This project brings together an observed workplace problem, nontrivial geometric reconstruction, a change in material behavior, additive manufacturing, and a field trial. The photographs and videos connect the replacement part to its actual machine application.

The original September 2023 account describes a 90-day trial and a $35 replacement versus an original $45–$65 price range. The source is my contemporaneous report, not an independent cost or reliability audit.

Return to selected contributions →

Why I made it

The fork holds a tool in the Multicam rack, so a replacement had to fit the holder and survive regular use. The original ABS forks kept breaking, and replacements were listed at $45 to $65 each. I decided to model the part myself and try a material with more flex.

Rebuilding the shape in Fusion

The original fork did not give me many easy square edges to measure from. I worked through the curved opening, mounting points, relieved areas, and changing outside shape until the model matched the part I needed to replace.

Why I switched to TPU

The original forks were ABS and kept breaking. I printed the replacement in TPU because I wanted some flex instead of another brittle part. The print used 15% infill and a 0.2 mm layer height on my Voron V0.2.

The 90-day update

After 90 days in the tool rack, the TPU fork was still working and the flex was doing what I hoped it would. I do not have machine-cycle counts or a formal life test, so that update is an observation from actual use.

The saved versions

I saved three versions in the repository: ToolFork_v1.3mf, ToolFork_v2.3mf, and ToolFork_v2_add4mm.3mf. They are revisions of the same tool-fork project, including the later 4 mm variation.

From broken part to working replacement

The original ABS forks in the Multicam rack kept breaking, and replacements were listed at $45 to $65 each. I measured the awkward shape, rebuilt it in Fusion, printed it in TPU on my Voron V0.2, and installed it in the machine. TPU gave the fork the flex I was looking for instead of repeating the brittle material. After 90 days it was still working, and I later made the dimensioned drawing and saved the different file revisions.
