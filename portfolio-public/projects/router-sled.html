I needed a router sled

I had been looking at a commercial router-sled kit, but I already had access to a CNC and could make the panels myself. I cut the support pieces and assembled the sled for use in the woodshop.

The CNC made the shop tool

This was a straightforward case of using one machine to build another tool. The CNC work was not the finished product; the assembled router sled was.

Starting from a useful reference

The idea came from the commercial kit I had been considering. I adapted that general arrangement to the material, machine, and hardware I had available.

Using the CNC to make another tool

I needed a router sled and had been looking at a commercial kit. Since I already had access to a CNC, I adapted the general arrangement, cut the support panels, and assembled the rails and router setup myself. The CNC work was only one step in the job. The finished result was another shop tool that could support later woodworking, which is why the assembled sled matters more here than the individual cut panels.
