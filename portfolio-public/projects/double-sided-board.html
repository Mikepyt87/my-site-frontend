A budget and a picture

The commission began with a budget and a supplied image, rather than a complete board specification. The original post describes the challenge of being asked to make an object in my own style.

Two faces, two intended uses

One face places walnut end grain in the center, bordered by long-grain maple, cherry, and zebrawood. The other uses teak, cherry, and black walnut for a serving face. A cherry frame and homemade walnut plugs carry the contrasting pattern around the edges.

A finished object, not just a wood sample

The photograph shows the design as an assembled board: a dark center, light dividing lines, and a framed perimeter. Material selection, composition, and fabrication are all visible in the same piece.

Working from an open brief

The customer gave me a budget and a reference image, but not a complete fabrication plan. I had to turn that into a board that worked from both sides: walnut for the chopping face, a contrasting serving face, a cherry frame, and homemade walnut plugs. I used several wood species and made the material arrangement part of the design. The open brief gave me freedom, but it also meant I had to make the choices hold together as one finished object.
